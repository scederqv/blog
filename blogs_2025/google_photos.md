# Google Photos: A Masterclass in Dark Patterns

Google started complaining that I'm running out of storage, which is fair enough. First I had a look at whatever emails I might have to clean up and the search turned up a bunch of emails with large attachments. The attachments are generally unimportant so I wanted to delete the attachments, but keep the emails but of course that's not possible.

## The Photo Problem

Next I turned to the biggest culprit, Google Photos. I don't exactly know how I've ended up with such a massive amount of photos in Google. Roughly half of the storage are legitimate albums that I've taken with my DSLR and published, but the rest are cell phone images and I don't recall having synced my iPhone with Google Photos in years, but apparently I have. Which is fine, I guess.

So I started going through that, most of it can probably be deleted. After all, I have all of it in Lightroom Classic (I don't buy storage from Adobe) and all the raw images, Lightroom catalogues and other files are being backed up to cheap S3 storage in the cloud anyway.

## The Deliberate UX Nightmare

And here I realized Google has systematically made this very, very hard to do on purpose. Their Google Drive is easily sortable by different attributes and you can even mount it locally. But the cleanup process removes all those features on the webpage. Instead you have a very short list of photos, where you can click "see more" every 10 rows or so, and you have to select each one you want to delete individually. They seem to be sorted randomly, or maybe by size, I don't know, but there's no way to select the way you even sort them, or a way to display a relevant amount of them in one page.

All other relevant UX factors are of course also missing such as select from here to here and things like that. No, you have to click each image separately and if you miss the little circle, you get to view it in a popup window.

**There can be no other interpretation**: They have deliberately gone out of their way to make the cleanup process as painful as possible to upsell more storage. As an aside, the storage they sell is a 2-month offer, after which the price will quadruple, or something. Maybe this works for them. I don't know.

## Why I Won't Pay for Frustrating Services

But why on earth would I buy a service that is hard to maintain? I'll happily pay for something that:

1. **Is reliable and works as expected**
2. **Is reasonably priced**
3. **Is the main use-case for whatever thing it's supposed to solve**

Google Photos meets **one** of those. It is reasonably priced, even if they try to lie about the price. It's still reasonably priced.

### Where Google Photos Fails

**It does not work as expected.** You can't maintain it. It's actually shittified on purpose.

**It is not the main use-case** for the thing it's supposed to do. It downscales the images so much that it's good for social media and such, but if I want to share pictures for print or even desktop backgrounds, I have to go somewhere else. It's fine for sharing cat pictures but nowhere near good enough for wedding photos.

## The Backwards Business Logic

So let's go back to the story. When faced with the problem they created for me, why would I choose to pay for storage? The idea here is to upsell storage because it's too frustrating to clean the storage. Why would I buy a frustrating product in the first place?

What's the business logic here really? Are people actually so stupid that this strategy works?
